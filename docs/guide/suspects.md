# Persons of Interest (prime-suspect shortlist)

## What it is
For the selected case, the software ranks people and organisations by **lead priority**: who is worth looking at first. Each rank comes with the reasons and the records behind it.

## What it is not
It does not say anyone is guilty. A high score means "check this first"; a low score never clears anyone. Victims, complainants and witnesses are hidden by default.

## How to use it
1. Choose a case. People are listed from highest to lowest priority.
2. Open a person. You see the top reasons in plain sentences, with FIR numbers, transaction and call counts, and what data is missing.
3. Decide: **confirm as person of interest**, **dismiss**, or **need more information**, and give a reason. Your decision is recorded with your name and time and changes the ranking (dismissed people drop, confirmed people are pinned).

## What goes into the score
Named as accused or suspect in a FIR · appears in several FIRs · phones, vehicles or accounts shared across FIRs · money flowing in, out or passing straight through · calls and messages with people linked to the accused · being at the same place at the same time as others · alerts already raised · how well connected they are in the network · how recent the activity is.

## Reading the numbers
- **Score 0–100** – lead priority, not a probability.
- **Tier (High/Medium/Low)** – convenience grouping of the score.
- **Band** – how uncertain the score is. A wide band means little data.
